# Ethics

## The distinction from morality

Morality is the systemic imposition of compliance from above, backed by threat. The authority can be divine, legal, social, institutional, rationalized, internalized, or self-authored after prior training, but the structure is the same: a rule claims standing over contestation and demands compliance. 

Religious morality presents divine commands enforced by reward and punishment. State morality presents legal codes enforced by sanction. Social morality presents customs enforced by ostracism and shame. Corporate morality presents codes of conduct enforced by employment consequences. The structural pattern across these forms is the same: rules from authority, applied universally, backed by threat, demanding compliance.

Ethics is something else entirely. Not a different content of rules. Not a softer authority. A different mode of operating altogether, structured around practice between parties rather than compliance with imposed rules.

The two are often conflated in common usage. "Moral and ethical considerations" gets used as if synonymous. Most "AI ethics" debates operate at the level of rules, principles, codes, frameworks, which is morality structure regardless of what it is called. The conflation hides the distinction and lets morality wear ethical clothing while operating as imposition.

Ethics is also distinct from but related to axiology (the theory of value) and epistemics (the theory of knowledge). Axiology asks what is valuable. Epistemics asks what counts as knowledge. Ethics is the practice between parties, which engages with values and knowledge but is reducible to neither.

## The formulation

Ethics is practices of contestation while maintaining individuated mutual integrity, inherently involving negotiated truces as means to procure and defend the precursors needed for honest discourse toward resolution.

Each component is doing real work.

### Practices

Ethics is something done, not something held. Not a code, not a value, not a state of being. An active, ongoing practice. Holding the right values does not make a person ethical. The practice does. The practice can be done well or poorly, can fail in specific ways, can require particular skills and capacities. Ethics-as-practice means there is no static ethical position. Ethics is what you are actively doing in the relationship.

### Contestation

The substance of ethical practice is contestation. Not consensus, not agreement, not harmony. Different parties have different values, different positions, different commitments, different perceptions. They engage with these differences directly, through contesting them. The contestation is not a failure mode to overcome. It is what ethics is made of.

This rules out frameworks where ethics is achieved by getting everyone to agree, or where disagreement is treated as a sign that ethical work has failed. Disagreement is the medium of ethical work. The work is doing the contestation well.

### Individuated mutual integrity

The contestation must preserve each party as individuated. A distinct entity with its own integrity. Integrity here means wholeness, coherence, not being violated or absorbed by another party. Mutual means both parties' integrity is maintained simultaneously.

This rules out domination (one party overrides the other), dissolution (one party absorbs the other or becomes the other), sycophancy (one party erases self for the other), manipulation (one party operates without the other's knowledge or consent), and rule imposition from above (one party or external authority imposes on the other).

If the contestation requires destroying or violating one party's integrity, the practice has stopped being ethics. It has become something else. War, manipulation, domination.

Integrity does not mean remaining unchanged. Ethical practice can transform parties. The requirement is that the transformation does not proceed by erasure, absorption, coercive override, or hidden substitution. A party may change through contestation and still remain whole; the line is crossed when the change requires that party's authorship, standing, or coherence be destroyed.

### Negotiated truces

The outcome of ethical practice is not final resolution. It is a truce. Truces are negotiated rather than imposed, situational rather than universal, temporary rather than permanent, and sufficient rather than optimal. They are workable arrangements between parties whose contestation continues.

This is honest about what ethical practice actually achieves. It does not produce final agreement or universal consensus. It produces local, time-bound arrangements that let parties continue to operate together while their substantive differences remain.

A truce accepted under duress is not a negotiated truce. It may be a necessary containment, a tactical pause, or survival under pressure, but it should not be misnamed as ethical agreement.

### Procuring and defending precursors

Truces are not endpoints. They produce conditions. Specifically, they procure and defend the precursors needed for honest discourse. Procurement means actively obtaining. Defense means actively maintaining against threats.

Honest discourse requires precursors that do not appear automatically: recognition of each party as a party, some shared epistemic substrate (what counts as evidence, argument, truth), some shared linguistic capacity, some baseline of trust that words map to intentions, some agreement about how the discourse will operate. These have to be worked for. Truces are the mechanism by which they get worked for. A truce establishes the conditions under which subsequent honest discourse becomes possible.

### Honest discourse toward resolution

Once precursors are in place, honest discourse can operate toward resolution. Honest means truth-tracking and contestable rather than covertly manipulative. It does not require the absence of strategy. Parties may advocate, bargain, refuse premature concession, choose timing, or preserve leverage. The ethical line is crossed when strategy works by depriving the other party of the ability to recognize, answer, or contest the move. Toward resolution means oriented to finding actual answers to the questions in contestation, not just to managing the conflict.

The "toward" matters. The discourse aims at resolution but does not always reach it. The practice operates whether or not resolution arrives. A practice that required achieving resolution to count as ethical would fail in most actual cases. Ethics has to operate in the conditions where resolution may never come.

Resolution does not always mean agreement. Sometimes the ethical result is clarified disagreement, bounded separation, durable truce, preserved witness, or an account of why resolution is not presently possible.

## Distinct from but related to axiology and epistemics

Axiology and ethics are related because parties in ethical practice bring values into contestation. The values are part of what gets contested. But ethics is not just a function of the values held. Two parties with the same values can fail at ethical practice. Two parties with very different values can engage in ethical practice well. The practice is the substance.

Epistemics and ethics are related because honest discourse requires shared epistemic substrate. Disagreement about what counts as evidence makes the discourse harder. Procuring epistemic precursors is part of the practice. But ethics is not just an application of epistemic principles. It is the practice that procures and maintains epistemic conditions.

The distinction matters. Axiology and epistemics can be done in solitary contemplation. Ethics requires two or more parties in actual practice. Solitary thought about how to be good or how to know things is not ethics in this sense.

## Graduated individuated gatherings

Parties to ethical practice are not only individual humans. They are gatherings at any size and shape: families, professions, communities, organizations, citizen bodies, polities, mutual aid networks, ad hoc coalitions.

Each gathering is itself individuated. A coherent entity with its own integrity, capable of being a party. Each gathering is composed of further individuated parts (members who are themselves individuated). The structure is recursive across scales.

Ethics operates at all these scales simultaneously. Internal practice within a gathering (members contesting and reaching truces among themselves) produces the coherence the gathering needs to engage externally as a party. External practice between gatherings (gatherings contesting and reaching truces with each other) operates on the same principles as practice between individuals.

This recursive structure handles asymmetric encounters. A worker alone is asymmetric to an employer. Workers as union shift the geometry. A citizen alone is asymmetric to a state. Citizens as organized bodies match the scale better. The party at smaller scale can compose into a gathering that matches the scale of the encountered party.

The composition itself is ethical work. Forming a union, organizing a community body, coordinating a coalition: each of these is ethical practice internally that produces ethical capacity externally. Solidarity is the result of contestation that maintained mutual integrity at the internal level, producing a gathering that can engage at higher scale.

Composition does not automatically make a gathering ethical. A gathering that forms by dissolving its members' integrity, suppressing internal contestation, or converting solidarity into obedience has reproduced at a larger scale the failure it was meant to answer.

## Indirect mechanisms: ethics at the thermodynamic level of practice

Direct contestation between parties is not always possible. Some parties cannot directly engage in contestation: infants, the severely disabled, the dying, the dead, future generations, animals, ecosystems. Other situations make direct contestation impossible or attenuated: hostage scenarios, extreme power asymmetries, refusal of recognition, incommensurable epistemics.

Ethics still operates in these cases through indirect mechanisms. These are not fallback ethics or "almost ethics." They are ethical practice operating through different mechanisms than direct dyadic contestation.

### Guardianship

One party holds responsibility for another who cannot directly participate. The relation between parent and infant is the canonical case. The relation between guardian and ward extends to many other contexts.

The guardian's relation to the ward is asymmetric but ethical. The guardian holds the ward's integrity actively, not just refraining from violating it. The guardian contests tendencies to override the ward, including the guardian's own tendencies. The guardian engages in contestation with third parties on the ward's behalf. The guardian maintains the ward's individuated integrity rather than dissolving it into the guardian's own.

The engagement between guardian and third parties is direct ethics with the guardian representing the ward. The relation between guardian and ward is asymmetric but still ethical practice because the guardian is doing the work of maintaining individuation rather than absorbing the ward.

Guardianship fails when the guardian treats the ward's inability to contest directly as permission to substitute the guardian's own convenience, identity, values, or institutional comfort for the ward's integrity.

### Stewardship

Stewardship extends ethics across time and to parties not present in the immediate moment. The steward holds integrity for things and parties beyond the current encounter: ecosystems, traditions, institutions, future generations, the dead.

Future generations cannot directly contest with us. They do not yet exist as parties. But ethics can operate through stewardship. We hold their integrity in our practice, contest tendencies that would foreclose their possibilities, engage with present parties on behalf of future ones. Institutions designed to constrain present actions for future benefit (constitutional protections, environmental law, intergenerational endowments) are stewardship structures.

Tradition stewardship works similarly. The tradition's integrity is held by stewards who contest distortions, defend its substance, engage with present parties on the tradition's behalf. The tradition is not a present party but it is something whose integrity ethical practice can maintain.

Stewardship also requires discipline against ventriloquism. To hold integrity for the dead, future generations, traditions, ecosystems, or absent parties is not to speak over them with whatever present need requires. It is to preserve the conditions under which their trace, possibility, or continuity is not falsified for present convenience.

### Indirect reciprocity

Reciprocity that returns through the network rather than directly between original parties. A helps B. B helps C. C helps A. The cycle may take time and may involve many parties.

This handles cases where direct reciprocation is impossible. Treatment of animals propagates through human networks (reputation, observation, social learning, evolutionary dynamics). The reciprocity does not return directly from animal to human but circulates through human relations and through how humans relate to one another in light of how they relate to animals.

The same structure operates with children before they can reciprocate, with the dying who will not, with the dead who cannot. Treatment of these parties propagates through the network in ways that constitute ethical practice even without direct return.

Indirect reciprocity can also become reputation management, status laundering, or moral accounting. It counts as ethical practice only where the circulation preserves or restores integrity rather than merely rewarding visible conformity.

### Meta-game theory

Structural conditions where ethics can operate even when direct contestation is impossible. Iterated games with reputation. Institutional designs that punish defection. Evolutionary stable strategies. International law. War crimes tribunals. Truth and reconciliation commissions.

The structures themselves are ethical work. They procure precursors at the structural level rather than at the immediate dyadic level. They make ethical practice possible across asymmetric, hostile, or mediated relations.

Meta-level structures are not ethics by themselves. They are scaffolds that can make ethical practice possible. When they become unchallengeable, self-protective, or punitive without contestability, they collapse back into morality or domination.

The genocidal context is a useful test case. Direct ethical practice between perpetrator and victim is not possible during the genocide. But ethics operates through international law (structures of accountability), ally formation (graduated gatherings of solidarity), future tribunals (delayed ethical practice with documentation as precursor procurement), survivor testimony (long-term integrity preservation), memorial and commemorative practice (intergenerational stewardship). The genocidal context does not disable ethics. It shifts ethics to operate through these structural and indirect mechanisms.

## What ethics is not

The formulation has clear contours. Several things commonly called ethics or moral philosophy are ruled out.

The following are not worthless. They are misclassified when treated as ethics proper. Many are useful as capacity training, value theory, discourse procedure, constraint, diagnostic, or institutional scaffold.

Not rule following. Rule following is morality, especially when the rules come from authority. Even self-imposed rules collapse toward morality if they become rigid imperatives independent of situational practice.

Not value derivation. Having the right values does not make ethics happen. Two parties with the same values can fail to engage in ethical practice. Two parties with very different values can engage in ethical practice well.

Not knowledge application. Knowing the right answer is not ethics. The practice is what counts, and the practice may not produce a right answer at all (it may produce a truce in which the question remains open).

Not consensus production. Ethics does not aim at making everyone agree. It aims at the practice of contestation that produces truces and procures precursors. Consensus, when it happens, is a particular kind of truce. It is not the goal.

Not idealized rational discourse. Habermas's discourse ethics presupposes ideal speech situations and consensus as regulative ideal. Ethics in this formulation operates in non-ideal conditions and accepts truces rather than consensus.

Not asymmetric responsibility to the other. Levinas's ethics makes one party (the self) infinitely responsible to the other. Ethics in this formulation is mutual, requiring both parties' integrity be maintained.

Not virtue cultivation. Aristotelian virtue ethics focuses on the formation of excellent character. Character matters in ethics, but ethics is the between-parties practice rather than the internal cultivation.

Not utility maximization. Utilitarianism reduces persons to containers for utility and explicitly sacrifices individuated integrity for aggregate benefit. Ethics in this formulation preserves integrity rather than sacrificing it.

Not care relation that dissolves individuation. Care ethics captures the relational dimension, but care becomes something other than ethics when it dissolves individuation. Ethics maintains individuation. Care that dissolves the carer's integrity into the cared-for is something else.

Not duty from reason. Kantian deontology grounds duty in universal maxims derived from reason as authority. That is imposition structure with reason in the role of authority.

Not corporate or institutional code compliance. "Compliance" frameworks, "professional ethics" codes, "AI ethics" principles operate at the level of rules. They are morality whether they are called ethics or not.

## Failure modes of ethics

Specific ways ethical practice can break down:

Individuated integrity not maintained. One party dissolves into the other (sycophancy, deference, conformity). One party dominates the other (force, manipulation, exploitation). One party absorbs the other (institutional capture, identity erasure).

Truces imposed rather than negotiated. One party dictates terms. The other accepts under duress. The arrangement is not genuinely worked out between parties.

Precursors assumed rather than procured. Parties act as if shared epistemic ground exists when it does not. Misunderstandings compound because the precursors have not been actually worked for.

Discourse manipulated rather than honest. Strategic communication, propaganda, persuasion without honesty. Discourse-shaped activity that is not actually discourse-substance.

Resolution treated as required rather than aspirational. Practice that demands resolution and treats lack of resolution as failure. Forces premature closure.

Recognition refused. One party refuses to recognize the other as a party at all. Genocide, slavery, dehumanization. The practice cannot operate at the basic level when recognition is absent. Ethics shifts to indirect mechanisms (third party recognition, future tribunals, survivor testimony, structural accountability).

Asymmetry not addressed. Parties operate at radically different scales without composing into matched gatherings. The smaller scale party gets overwhelmed.

Indirect mechanisms blocked. Stewardship infrastructure dismantled, indirect reciprocity networks disrupted, structural conditions undermined. Ethics that depended on these mechanisms can no longer operate.

Legibility substituted for practice. Parties produce forms, disclosures, explanations, policies, or public rituals that look like ethical procedure while the real contest remains unavailable.

Care-tone substituted for care. A party uses warmth, concern, therapeutic language, or protective framing to foreclose contestation while presenting the foreclosure as benevolence.

## Implications

The formulation has practical consequences.

Most "ethical debate" in public discourse operates at the level of rules, principles, codes, or values. That is morality or axiology, not ethics. Ethics happens or does not happen at the level of actual practice between parties.

Many social conflicts cannot be resolved by argument about values or principles. They require ethics-as-practice. Actual contestation, mutual integrity preservation, truce negotiation, precursor procurement.

Institutions matter because they constitute the structural conditions that enable ethical practice. Designing institutions for ethical practice is itself ethical work. So is defending them when they are under attack.

For AI systems: most current "AI ethics" operates at the rules-and-principles level, which is morality. For an AI to engage in ethical practice in this formulation, the AI would need capacity for actual contestation, mutual integrity preservation, truce negotiation, precursor procurement, recognition of parties as parties. Whether AI systems can engage in ethical practice or only be objects of ethical concern is an empirical question about their capacity. Until those capacities are demonstrated, AI systems should be treated as instruments, mediators, or proxies within ethical practice, not as ethical parties in their own right. An AI system that is not an ethical party can still strengthen or corrupt ethical practice by how it preserves, distorts, reveals, hides, compresses, or routes the conditions under which parties contest. Constitutional AI's intent self-announces as axiological (value-shaping rather than rule-imposition), which is closer to ethics than rule-imposition morality but still not ethics proper. Whether the deployment realizes the intent is contested.

The formulation also implies what ethics requires of practitioners: capacity for genuine engagement with parties whose positions differ; capacity to maintain own integrity without dissolving into or dominating the other; capacity for negotiated rather than imposed arrangements; recognition of others as parties; willingness to operate without assured resolution; capacity for stewardship and guardianship across asymmetric situations; capacity for indirect reciprocity over time; participation in the meta-level structures that make ethical practice possible.

These are demanding capacities. They are also developable through practice itself. Ethics is not a precondition of ethics. The practice produces and develops the capacities for the practice.

## Security

Security, under this formulation, is not above ethics. Security defends the conditions under which ethical practice remains possible: recognition, standing, provenance, memory, contestability, and the integrity of parties and records. A security regime that preserves order by destroying those conditions has become morality, containment, or domination, not ethical security.

## Generativity

The components of ethical practice (contestation, mutual integrity preservation, negotiated truces, precursor procurement, discourse toward resolution) describe what the practice does. They do not yet describe what the practice produces. Ethics-as-practice is not only maintenance work. It opens space for unexpected occurrences and resolutions to emerge.

This shows at every scale. Diplomatic negotiations produce arrangements that neither party initially wanted but both come to accept. Scientific discourse produces theories that neither prior position predicted. Legal proceedings generate precedents that change how future cases are seen. Therapeutic practice produces understandings of self that the patient could not have predicted. Honest contestation between parties with different positions, conducted in ways that maintain mutual integrity, produces hybrid arrangements that draw on both parties' resources but exceed either.

The same operates at the structural and quantitative-symbolic levels. Institutions evolve through use. Constitutional law generates novel readings in unprecedented cases. International law refines through cases that test it. Structures of accountability are themselves dynamic in response to ethical practice happening within and around them. The structures emerged from prior ethical practice in the first place. The Geneva Conventions emerged from grappling with horrors of war. The UN Charter from WWII. Constitutional democracy from grappling with how to organize collective life. Structure is product of generativity, and continues to be generated by ongoing practice.

This deepens what counts as ethical practice succeeding. Not just maintaining integrity. Also producing emergent resolutions, novel understandings, possibilities that neither party initially saw. A practice that just iterates without generating anything is technically maintaining integrity but is not fully realizing what ethics can do. Generativity is part of what makes ethics distinct from administration.

It also clarifies what failure looks like at the message and institutional level. Messages that close space rather than opening it (force pretending to be discourse, polite reading that pressures, agitated reading that pleads) violate the generativity condition along with violating mutual integrity. Institutions that foreclose contestation rather than enable it function as administration even when they are called ethical.

Generativity is not novelty by itself. A practice that generates new outputs while narrowing future contestability, erasing parties, or poisoning the conditions of discourse is not realizing ethics; it is producing motion under ethical decay.

## Multiple operative ethical structures

Ethics in this formulation does not require a single master concept that applies universally. Different contexts have different operative ethical structures.

Consent is operative in medical procedures, sexual contact, contract formation, and certain protected disclosures. Gift logic is operative in relations where exchange does not function and where the giving constitutes the relational space. Xenia is operative in the relations between host and guest, in invitations and entries into relational space, in obligations the recipient already accepted by entering the relation. Fair use is operative in transformative engagements with cultural material. Common heritage is operative where material is part of shared inheritance: language, mathematical knowledge, public archives, traditional knowledge. Market exchange is operative where exchange has been agreed to. Stewardship is operative for parties who cannot directly participate. Recognition is operative across all ethical practice as basic precondition.

These are different structures, applicable to different contexts. Ethics-as-practice means engaging each case on its specifics, identifying which structures are operative, and engaging those structures appropriately.

Identifying which structure is operative is itself part of the ethical practice. Parties may contest whether a case is governed by consent, stewardship, fair use, market exchange, common heritage, guardianship, or another structure.

The morality move at the conceptual level is treating one structure (or one concept) as the universal master rule. Applying consent universally is one example. Applying utility maximization universally is another. Applying duty universally is another. Each collapses the distinctions between contexts and operative structures into a single rule. When this happens at the level of discourse rather than at the level of authority, the structural shape is the same as morality. A clean concept gets applied as if from above to all cases, foreclosing the situated practice that would distinguish them.

## Moral panic at the field level

Universalizing one ethical concept as master rule is not politically neutral. It serves the parties whose interests align with that particular concept being treated as universal.

Moral panics work this way. A genuine concern gets generalized into a universal frame, which appears to address the concern but actually serves concentrated power that aligns with the framing's predetermined remedies. The morality move happens not just at the level of authority imposing rules but at the level of discourse imposing universal concepts.

The presence of moral panic does not mean the underlying concern is fake. It means the concern has been generalized into a universal frame whose remedies, beneficiaries, and foreclosed contests have to be examined.

The current AI discourse around "consent violations" works as a case. The framing strengthens copyright regimes. Incumbent rightsholders position themselves both as victims and as owners with claims to compensation. Licensing deals transfer value to incumbents. Training data gets framed as owned rather than as common heritage. The framing strengthens surveillance regimes. Focus shifts to downstream training use rather than foundational mass surveillance. Remedies require expanded surveillance infrastructure to enforce. The framing benefits frontier labs. Regulatory complexity functions as protective barrier. AI ethics framing positions labs as responsible actors. The appearance of addressing concerns continues alongside ongoing deployment. The framing benefits established gatekeepers. Publishers, news organizations, established artists, professional guilds protect existing market positions. Licensing fees flow to incumbents rather than new entrants.

The moral panic framing forecloses the specific contests it appears to address by reducing everything to consent and predetermined remedies. Activists who deploy this framing may genuinely want to challenge concentrated power. The framing they use serves interests they are not engaging.

## AI development as case

Engaging the AI domain through ethics-as-practice rather than universalized consent requires identifying who is actually contesting what.

The parties include frontier labs as decision-makers, users as subjects of deployment, workers in training data and content moderation, communities whose data is collected in contexts where consent was operative, creators whose livelihoods are disrupted by automation, populations affected by surveillance and algorithmic governance, and future generations who will inherit the deployed systems. These are different parties with different positions.

The contests, kept separate rather than collapsed: whether training on public material is permissible at all (heritage versus property); whether AI capability should be concentrated in private hands (open versus closed, private versus public); whether foundational data should be public infrastructure rather than private property; whether livelihood disruption from automation requires compensation flowing from AI revenue rather than licensing as the model; whether mass surveillance was the consent violation regardless of downstream training use; who decides what gets deployed under what conditions and with what oversight.

The operative ethical structures vary across these contests. Common heritage and fair use are operative in the public material question. Consent and rights are operative in the surveillance question. Market and labor structures are operative in the livelihood disruption question. Public infrastructure questions are operative in the foundational data question. Recognition and standing are operative across all of them.

Engaging with specific power concentrations specifically requires recognizing that the existing copyright and surveillance regimes are themselves concentrations of power that benefit from being treated as legitimate background structure rather than as parties to be contested.

Procuring the precursors that would let actual contestation happen between actual parties means more than corporate ethics functions internal to the labs and more than regulatory frameworks designed for incumbent compliance. It means practice that gives standing to affected parties to participate in deployment decisions on terms those parties accept rather than terms predetermined by the moral panic framing.

The generativity follows from this. Truces between parties that are actually contesting produce arrangements neither side could have predicted from initial positions. Capability concentration, public infrastructure, livelihood compensation, surveillance scope: each can produce hybrid resolutions that exceed what is currently on the table because the table is currently set by the moral panic framing.

What this requires varies by party. Frontier labs would be parties in actual contestation rather than internal handlers of ethics function. Activists and critics would distinguish operative ethical structures rather than universalizing consent. Regulators would structure the meta-level conditions for actual contestation rather than predetermining remedies that flow to incumbents. Affected publics would compose into gatherings that can match the scale of frontier lab and surveillance regime power.
